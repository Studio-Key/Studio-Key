# Voice and customer insight

Source: Rich's help@studio-key.co.uk mailbox (about 20 threads, mid-2025 to Oct 2026), read-only, anonymised. Use alongside `.claude/skills/studio-key-hosting/SKILL.md`.

## How Rich writes

- Greets by first name: "Hi Jon", "Morning Jon", "Good afternoon Sarah". Sometimes a short personal line ("Hope you've recovered from the school holidays!").
- Signs off "R." or "Kind regards, Rich". Sometimes "Speak soon!"
- Routine replies run one to three sentences. Important ones are long, structured, numbered, with exact figures.
- Relaxed British English: "Oof", "Blimey", "painfully busy".
- Explains tech with everyday comparisons, then splits the site into hosting, email and domain.
- Leads with the outcome: "Everything is stable." Then: "Nothing to do from your end."
- Honest and on the customer's side: "to be fully transparent", "Absolutely no pressure from my end", "there's no financial gain for you".
- Owns delays without fuss: "Thanks for your patience, a very busy few weeks!"
- Gives a price and a time estimate together: "about 1 hour so £65."

### Phrases worth echoing on the site

- "No problem at all"
- "Nothing to do from your end"
- "to be fully transparent with you"
- "Absolutely no pressure from my end"
- "I never recommend moving a domain provider"
- "so your emails carry on as normal"

### Turning his voice into web copy

- Keep the warmth and directness. Drop the typos, the capitals for emphasis and the in-jokes.
- Customers sometimes miss the point of long technical emails ("I can't claim to understand everything in that email"). Web copy should give the gist first, detail second.

## Pain points seen in real emails

- An old host "looking to offload his smaller sites", leaving the owner "in limbo", then chasing them to move.
- A neglected site: PHP 7.2, a theme unsupported since 2016, plugins that could not update, 17,000 spam comments.
- A £178 "domain renewal notification" invoice from a third party.
- A previous developer's CDN locking DNS, with a hard deadline to move.
- Owners not knowing who hosts their site, email or domain. Old agencies still listed as admins.

No SSL-fee complaints or downtime figures turned up in the mailbox. The "£79+" claim in the homepage copy needs a source (see PRD open items).

## Questions customers ask (FAQ source)

- Do I have to move my domain or email to you?
- Can you move my site from my current host? What happens to my emails?
- Is this renewal notice genuine? Who holds my domain?
- Has the security fix been applied? Is my site safe?
- Can I pay yearly?
- How do I set up my email on my phone?
- How do I leave, how much notice, and what happens to my files?
- How much will this fix cost and how long will it take?
- Can I give my marketing agency access?
